How to Verify an Opportunity Before You Apply: A 10-Point Checklist

Hearing about an opportunity in a WhatsApp group or on social media is exciting, but forwarded posts are often old, changed or fake. Spending ten minutes verifying a listing can protect your money, your data and your time. Use this checklist before every application.

By the Naija Opportunity Hub editorial team · Updated 26 November 2026

The 10-point checklist

  1. Find the official source. Search for the organisation's own website and find the same announcement there. If you cannot find it, treat it with suspicion.
  2. Check the web address. Make sure it matches the organisation's real domain exactly. Look for swapped letters, extra words or unusual endings.
  3. Compare the details. The title, deadline, eligibility and benefits should match the official announcement word for word.
  4. Check the date. Old opportunities are often reposted as new. Confirm that the deadline has not passed.
  5. Test the application link. It should lead to the organisation's own site or a recognised application system, not a free form with a personal account.
  6. Look at the email address. Official contacts normally use the organisation's own domain.
  7. Look for fees. If anyone asks you to pay to apply, be selected or "secure your slot", stop. See our guide to fake offers.
  8. Search for reports. Search the name with "scam", "fake" or "review" and read what other people say.
  9. Check the organisation's presence. Genuine organisations have a history: a known address, active official social media pages, press mentions or a registration you can confirm.
  10. Ask someone you trust. A lecturer, a career adviser or a colleague in the sector can often tell you quickly if something looks wrong.

Protect your information

  • Share only what is necessary at each stage. A first application rarely needs your bank details or ID numbers.
  • Use a strong, unique password for application portals and turn on two-step verification on your email.
  • Be careful with documents. If you must send a passport scan or certificate, add a note about what it is for.

Opportunities shared on social media

Posts spread fast, and each share can change the details. Before you share or apply, go to the original source. When you do share an opportunity, include the official link so that others can check it themselves.

How Naija Opportunity Hub checks listings

We open the organisation's official page, check that the title, deadline and application link match and record the date of the check. A listing marked Verified has passed those checks on that date, while one marked Not verified yet has not been completed. Read the full process on our verification page. Even then, always confirm details on the official website before you apply, because requirements can change.

Report what you find

If you find an offer that looks wrong, tell us using the "Report a problem" link on the listing or our contact page. Your report helps other applicants stay safe.

Frequently asked questions

What is the fastest check I can do?

Go to the organisation's own website and look for the same announcement. If it is not there, do not apply.

Is a Verified label a guarantee?

No. It means we checked the official source on the date shown. Deadlines and rules can change, so confirm on the official site.

What if I already shared my details?

Change your passwords, watch for unusual messages, tell your bank if banking details were shared and report the offer.
