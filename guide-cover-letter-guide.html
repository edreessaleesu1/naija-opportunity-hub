How to Write a Cover Letter That Gets Read

A cover letter is your chance to explain why you are the right person for a specific role. Many applicants skip it or send a generic letter, which is exactly why a good one helps you stand out. This guide gives you a simple structure that works for jobs, internships and graduate programmes.

Why it matters

Your CV shows what you have done. Your cover letter explains why it matters for this employer. Hiring managers use it to judge your communication, motivation and understanding of the role, so a few well-chosen paragraphs can lift an average CV.

The four-paragraph structure

Paragraph 1: Why you are writing

State the position you are applying for and where you saw it, then give one sentence that shows why you are a strong fit. Example: "I am applying for the Graduate Trainee role advertised on your careers page. I recently completed a BSc in Accounting and led a student finance project that saved our society 15 per cent of its annual budget."

Paragraph 2: Your strongest evidence

Choose two or three achievements that match the job requirements and explain them briefly with results. Do not copy your whole CV. Show that you understand what the employer needs and how you can provide it.

Paragraph 3: Why this organisation

Name something specific about the company: a project, a value, a product or a recent achievement, and explain why it interests you. This proves your application is not a mass mailing.

Paragraph 4: Close politely

Thank the reader, say you would welcome an interview and give your contact details. Keep the tone confident, not desperate.

Writing tips

  • Keep it to one page, around 250 to 350 words.
  • Address a person when you can find the name. If not, "Dear Hiring Manager" is acceptable.
  • Use plain language. Short sentences are easier to read and more convincing than long formal phrases.
  • Be specific. Replace "good communication skills" with an example of a presentation you delivered or a team you led.
  • Match the tone to the employer. A bank or law firm expects a formal tone. A start-up may welcome a more relaxed style.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Sending the same letter to every employer.
  • Starting every sentence with "I".
  • Repeating the CV line by line.
  • Mentioning the wrong company name, which is surprisingly common when letters are reused.
  • Typing errors. Read the letter aloud before you send it.
  • Complaining about a previous employer.

Sending it by email

Put a clear subject line such as "Application for Graduate Trainee, Your Name". Paste a short version of the letter in the email body or attach it as a PDF, and attach your CV. Name files clearly, for example "Amina-Bello-CV.pdf".

Frequently asked questions

Do I need a cover letter if the job ad does not ask for one?

It is usually worth sending one, because it can set you apart. If the portal does not accept one, add a short note in the email.

How long should it be?

One page, roughly 250 to 350 words.

Can I use a template?

Use a template for layout, but write the content in your own words and customise every letter for the employer.
