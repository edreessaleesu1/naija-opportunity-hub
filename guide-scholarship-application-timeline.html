International Scholarship Applications: A 6-Month Preparation Timeline

Competitive international scholarships are won by applicants who start early. Rushed applications have weak essays, missing documents and late references. This guide gives you a realistic six-month plan that you can adapt to your own deadline.

How to use this plan

Count backwards from the application deadline. If you only have three months, combine steps, but keep the order. Always confirm the dates and requirements on the official scholarship website, because they change each year.

Six months before: research and decide

  • List scholarships that match your level, subject and country of study. Note the eligibility rules, the closing date and what they fund.
  • Shortlist three to five realistic options and one or two backup plans.
  • Check whether you need an admission offer first or can apply to the scholarship at the same time.
  • Book an English language test if one is required, because test centres in Nigerian cities fill up.

Five months before: documents and tests

  • Request your official transcripts and certificates early. Universities can take weeks to process these.
  • Locate your NYSC certificate or exemption letter, and your passport, or start the passport process, which can take time.
  • Take your English test, so that you can retake it if needed before the deadline.

Four months before: strengthen your profile

  • Look for leadership and community experience you can still build or document: a project, a volunteer role, a society position.
  • Gather evidence of your results: letters, certificates, photos, figures.
  • Identify and approach your referees, using the advice in our referee guide.

Three months before: first drafts

  • Write the first draft of your personal statement and essays, following our personal statement guide.
  • Update your CV. Our Scholarship CV template is a good starting point.
  • Check the course or university requirements, and write down any questions to ask the scholarship office.

Two months before: feedback and refinement

  • Ask at least two people to read your drafts: a lecturer, a past scholar or a mentor.
  • Rewrite based on their comments and cut anything that does not support your story.
  • Send your referees the full briefing pack and the deadline.

Final month: complete and submit

  • Fill in the online form in a draft state and check every field.
  • Scan documents clearly and in the requested format and size.
  • Confirm that references have been submitted.
  • Submit at least three to five days early. Servers slow down near deadlines and power or network failures are common.
  • Save a copy of everything you submitted and the confirmation message.

After you submit

Prepare for a possible interview by reviewing your application and practising answers. Keep your email and phone active, and check your spam folder. If you are not selected, ask for feedback where it is offered and use it for the next round.

Frequently asked questions

What if I have less than six months?

Keep the same order but compress the steps. Start with documents, English tests and referees, because they depend on other people.

When should I contact my referees?

As early as possible, at least four to six weeks before the deadline.

Should I apply to several scholarships?

Yes, as long as each application is tailored. A smaller number of strong, specific applications is better than many generic ones.
