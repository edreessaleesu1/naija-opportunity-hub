How to Write a CV for Nigerian Employers: Format, Length and What to Include

Recruiters in Nigeria often receive hundreds of CVs for a single role and spend less than a minute on each. A clear, honest and well-organised CV gets you through that first look. This guide explains the format, what to include and what to leave out.

Length and layout

  • Aim for one to two pages. Graduates and early-career applicants should usually keep to one or two pages. Longer CVs are normal only in academic and senior roles.
  • Use a clean, readable font such as Calibri, Arial or similar, in size 10 to 12, with clear section headings.
  • Keep the layout simple. Heavy graphics, tables and text boxes can confuse the applicant tracking systems (ATS) that many large employers use to scan CVs.
  • Save as PDF unless the employer asks for a different format, so your layout stays the same on every device.

The sections to include

Contact details

Your full name, phone number, a professional email address (for example, firstname.lastname@gmail.com) and your city. A LinkedIn link is helpful if your profile is complete.

Professional summary

Two to four lines that say who you are, what you offer and what you are looking for. Tailor it to each job.

Work experience and internships

List roles starting with the most recent. For each, include the job title, organisation, location and dates, followed by three to five bullet points. Start each bullet with an action verb and, where you can, include a result: "Reduced customer complaint response time from 48 to 24 hours".

Education

Include your degree, institution, year and class of degree where it helps you. Add relevant projects or final-year research if you have limited work experience.

Skills

List skills that match the job: software, languages, technical tools and relevant soft skills. Be honest, as you may be tested on them.

Certifications, volunteering and referees

Add short courses, professional certificates and volunteering that support your application. Referees can be listed or marked "available on request".

Nigerian specifics

  • NYSC. If you have completed National Youth Service, state it with the year and your place of primary assignment. If you are currently serving or about to serve, say so, because some employers care about this.
  • Photograph. A photo is not required by most employers. Include one only if the advertisement asks for it, and use a plain, professional image. Our Career Kit has several templates with an optional photo that stays on your device.
  • Personal details. Date of birth, marital status, religion and state of origin are generally not needed, and many employers do not expect them. Leave them out unless the instructions ask for them.
  • Professional email. Avoid nicknames in your email address.

Tailor it to each job

Read the job description and pick out the key requirements. Make sure the same words appear in your CV, naturally and truthfully. Move the most relevant experience to the top of the page. A CV that mirrors the advertisement scores better in both ATS scans and human reviews.

Common mistakes

  • Long paragraphs instead of short bullet points.
  • Listing duties instead of achievements.
  • Spelling mistakes and inconsistent dates.
  • Using the same CV for every job.
  • Exaggerating or inventing experience. Employers check, and being caught can cost you the offer.

Frequently asked questions

Should I put a photo on my CV?

Only if the job advertisement asks for it. Most employers do not need one, and a photo can make some automated systems struggle.

How far back should my work history go?

Usually the last ten years or your last three to four roles. Earlier jobs can be summarised in one line.

Is a one-page CV better?

For graduates with limited experience, one page is often enough. Use two pages if you have more relevant experience to show.
