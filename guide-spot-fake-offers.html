How to Spot a Fake Job or Scholarship Offer in Nigeria

Every week, thousands of Nigerians lose money, personal data or both to fake jobs and scholarships. The offers look professional and use real logos, so it is easy to be fooled when you are hoping for good news. This guide explains the warning signs, the most common scams and what to do if something feels wrong.

The warning signs

Real organisations behave in predictable ways. Scammers usually break at least one of these rules.

  • They ask you to pay to be considered. Genuine employers and scholarship providers do not charge "processing", "training", "registration" or "visa" fees to select you. A small number of legitimate processes have fees, such as a university application fee or an official English test, but these are paid on the institution's own website, never into a personal bank account or to a person on WhatsApp.
  • The email address does not match the organisation. A message from a company that comes from a free address such as Gmail or Yahoo, or from a look-alike domain with a small spelling change, is a warning sign.
  • They contact you first on WhatsApp or Telegram. Real recruiters usually reply from an official email or a company system. A job offer that arrives in a private chat, with no interview, is suspicious.
  • They promise a guaranteed result. No honest organisation can guarantee that you will win a scholarship or be hired before reviewing your application.
  • There is pressure to act quickly. Messages such as "pay in the next 2 hours or lose your slot" are designed to stop you from checking.
  • They ask for sensitive details too early. Your BVN, ATM card details, banking passwords or one-time codes are never needed to apply for a job or scholarship.
  • The offer is too good. A very high salary for little work, or a scholarship you never applied for, is rarely real.

Common scams to watch for

The "fee to confirm your place" scam

You apply (or are told you have been selected) and then asked to pay for a medical, uniform, training or "clearance". After you pay, they ask for more money, then disappear.

The fake recruitment agent

Someone claims to work with a large company or embassy and promises a visa-sponsored job abroad for a fee. Real sponsored jobs come from the employer after a proper interview process.

The scholarship you did not apply for

An email says you have won a scholarship from an organisation you have never heard of. The goal is to collect your documents, your identity details or an "acceptance fee".

Cloned websites

Scammers copy a real organisation's website and change the address slightly. The page looks identical, but the application form sends your details to them.

How to check an offer yourself

  1. Open the organisation's real website by typing the address yourself or searching for it, not by clicking the link in the message.
  2. Look for the same opportunity on that site's careers or scholarships page.
  3. Compare the email domain in the message with the domain on the official website.
  4. Search the organisation's name with the words "scam" or "fake" to see whether others have reported it.
  5. Ask a friend, a lecturer or a career adviser. A second opinion costs nothing.

If you have already been targeted

  • Stop all contact and do not send more money or documents.
  • If you paid, tell your bank straight away. Some payments can be stopped if you report quickly.
  • If you shared passwords, change them and turn on two-step verification.
  • Keep screenshots of the messages, account numbers and phone numbers. You can report fraud to the police or to the Economic and Financial Crimes Commission (EFCC).
  • Warn others in your WhatsApp groups and social pages so that fewer people are caught.

Frequently asked questions

Is it ever normal to pay for a scholarship or job application?

Rarely. Some universities charge an application fee and some tests such as IELTS have a fee, but these are paid through the official institution's website. You should never pay a recruiter, an agent or a personal account to be selected.

What if the offer uses the real company's logo?

Logos are easy to copy. Always check the web address and email domain and confirm the offer on the organisation's own website.

Where can I report a scam?

You can report to the police or the Economic and Financial Crimes Commission, tell your bank if you paid, and report suspicious listings to us through the contact page.
