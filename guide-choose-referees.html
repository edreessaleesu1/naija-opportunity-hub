How to Choose Referees and Ask for a Strong Reference Letter

Many job and scholarship applications ask for two or three referees, and a strong reference can tip a close decision. A weak or late one can do the opposite. This guide shows you who to ask, how to ask and how to help your referee write a letter that works for you.

Who should you ask?

Choose people who know your work well and can speak honestly about it. A strong referee is usually one of the following:

  • A lecturer or project supervisor who graded your work or supervised your research.
  • A current or former manager who saw how you perform at work.
  • A senior person from a volunteer or community project where you held responsibility.

For academic and scholarship applications, at least one referee should usually be academic. For jobs, at least one should be a manager or supervisor. Check the instructions, because some programmes specify who may write.

Who not to ask

  • Family members or close friends, unless the application explicitly allows it.
  • Famous or very senior people who barely know you. A detailed letter from someone who knows you is better than a short note from someone important.
  • Anyone who would not be able to say something positive and specific.

How to ask

  1. Ask early. Give at least four to six weeks. Busy lecturers and managers write many letters.
  2. Ask politely and make it easy to say no. "Would you be comfortable writing a strong reference for me?" gives them a fair way to decline, which is better than a lukewarm letter.
  3. Ask in person or by email, then follow up with the details in writing.

What to give your referee

Send one email with everything they need so they do not have to chase you:

  • The name of the scholarship or job, the deadline and how the letter must be submitted (link, email or sealed envelope).
  • Your up-to-date CV and a short summary of the application.
  • Three to five points you would like them to cover, for example the project you did together, your result and a skill they saw you use.
  • Any special form or word limit.

It is normal and helpful to remind them of specific things you did in their class or team, because memories fade.

Follow up and say thanks

  • Send a polite reminder about a week before the deadline.
  • Confirm with the application portal that the reference has arrived, if it lets you check.
  • Thank your referee and tell them the outcome. They will remember this when you need help again.

Extra tips

  • Use the same names and spellings in your application as those in the reference.
  • Keep a list of past referees with their current contact details and update it regularly.
  • Never write your own reference and ask someone to sign it unless the institution clearly allows this. It can be treated as dishonesty.

Frequently asked questions

How many referees do I need?

Usually two or three. Check the instructions and ask each referee in advance.

What if I graduated many years ago?

Use recent work supervisors where possible, and an old lecturer only if they still remember you well and can comment on your work.

Can my referee be a friend?

Normally no. Choose someone who has supervised or taught you.
