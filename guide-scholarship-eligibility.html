Understanding Scholarship Eligibility: Degree Class, Work Experience, Age Limits and English Tests

Before you spend weeks on an application, make sure you are eligible. Many strong applicants are rejected because they missed a basic rule. This guide explains the eligibility conditions you will meet most often and how to check them properly.

Where to find the real rules

The official scholarship website and the application guidance document are the only reliable sources. Read the eligibility section completely, including the footnotes and the frequently asked questions. If something is unclear, email the scholarship office before the deadline. Rules differ by year and by country, so never rely only on an old post or a friend's memory.

Degree class and grades

Many scholarships ask for a strong first degree, commonly a second class upper division or equivalent, although some accept second class lower with other strengths, and some are open to all classes. Postgraduate scholarships often ask for a first degree before you start. If your result is lower than the preferred level, look for programmes that value experience, leadership or research potential, and explain special circumstances honestly.

Work experience

Some scholarships, particularly for master's study aimed at emerging leaders, require a minimum amount of work experience, which may include internships, volunteering and NYSC service, depending on the rules. Others are for fresh graduates. Check how the scholarship counts experience: whether it must be full time, paid or in a specific field.

Age limits

Some programmes, particularly those for young leaders or certain government schemes, have age limits. Others have none. If you are close to an age limit, check the exact date on which age is calculated.

English language tests

  • Universities and scholarships commonly ask for IELTS, TOEFL or an equivalent test, usually with minimum scores.
  • Some institutions waive the test if your degree was taught in English, but this varies, and a waiver letter from your university may be required.
  • Test results usually expire after about two years, so check the date.
  • For some scholarships, you must have the test result by a specific date, or only by the time you begin the course.

Nationality and residence

Scholarships are often restricted to citizens of certain countries, for example Commonwealth countries, African countries or developing economies. Some require you to live in your home country when applying. Check this before you invest time.

Return-home conditions

Several scholarships expect you to return to your home country for a period after your studies, so that your skills benefit the place you come from. Read the terms carefully and make sure you can accept them. Your application should show a realistic plan for how you will use your studies in Nigeria or in Africa.

Documents you may need

  • Degree certificates and transcripts, sometimes with certified copies.
  • International passport with enough validity.
  • NYSC certificate or exemption.
  • English test result or waiver letter.
  • CV, personal statement and reference letters.
  • Admission letter, if required before or after the scholarship decision.

If you are not sure you qualify

Email the scholarship team with a short, clear question and the specific detail you are unsure about. Keep the reply. If you still do not meet the criteria, move on to the next option and consider building the missing piece, such as work experience or a test score, for the next year.

Find opportunities that fit. Browse current scholarships, internships and jobs on our opportunities page and check the eligibility section of each listing.

Frequently asked questions

Can I apply if I do not meet one requirement?

It depends on the scholarship. If it is a firm rule, your application may be rejected. Email the scholarship office to ask before you apply.

Do I need IELTS if my degree was taught in English?

Sometimes you can get a waiver, but not always. Check the rule for each programme.

Does NYSC count as work experience?

Some scholarships accept it and some do not. Read the definition of work experience in the guidance.
