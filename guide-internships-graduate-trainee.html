How to Apply for Internships and Graduate Trainee Programmes in Nigeria

Your first professional experience matters more than almost anything else on a young CV. Internships and graduate trainee programmes are the most common way into large employers, and they are competitive. This guide explains how they work in Nigeria and how to improve your chances.

By the Naija Opportunity Hub editorial team · Updated 26 November 2026

Types of opportunity

  • SIWES and industrial training. Students on many courses complete the Students Industrial Work Experience Scheme as part of their degree. Choose your placement thoughtfully, because it can become your first CV entry.
  • Internships. Short placements, usually for a few weeks or months, for students and recent graduates. They may be paid or unpaid, so read the terms.
  • Graduate trainee programmes. Structured entry programmes run by banks, oil and gas companies, consumer goods firms, consultancies and some public bodies. They usually include training, rotations and a route to a permanent role.
  • Fellowships and volunteering. Programmes run by NGOs and development organisations that give practical experience and networks.

Where to look

  • The careers pages of the organisations you are interested in. Many graduate programmes open once a year, so check for dates and set reminders.
  • Verified listings on Naija Opportunity Hub and your university's careers or alumni office.
  • Professional networks such as LinkedIn, and your lecturers' and alumni contacts.
  • Industry associations in your field.

How to prepare

A focused CV

If you do not have work experience, show projects, volunteering, leadership in student societies, relevant coursework and skills. Follow our CV guide and build one with our Graduate template.

A tailored cover letter

Explain why you want this programme at this organisation, using our cover letter guide.

Practise for the assessment stages

Graduate programmes often use online tests, group exercises and panel interviews. Practise numerical and verbal reasoning, learn to contribute clearly in groups and prepare examples using the STAR method in our interview guide.

Ways to stand out

  • Start early. Many applicants apply in the last hours.
  • Show curiosity about the industry: follow its news, and mention a trend or challenge in your application.
  • Gain small practical experience first, such as a short course, a freelance task or a campus project.
  • Network politely. A short, specific message to someone in the field asking for advice often gets a reply.

NYSC and timing

Many graduate programmes ask whether you have completed or been exempted from NYSC, and some accept applicants who are about to serve. Read the eligibility rules and have your certificate or call-up letter ready.

Paid, unpaid and fake offers

An unpaid internship may be legitimate, but you should still know what you will learn and for how long. Never pay to get an internship or to join a trainee programme. See our guide on how to spot fake offers before you share your documents.

Frequently asked questions

When do graduate trainee programmes open?

Many open once a year, often in the second half of the year, but dates vary. Check each employer's careers page and set reminders.

Can I apply before I finish my degree?

Some programmes accept final-year students and others require a completed degree. Read the eligibility rules.

Are unpaid internships acceptable?

Some are legitimate, but ask about the duties, duration and what you will learn. Never pay for a placement.
